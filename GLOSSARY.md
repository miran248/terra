# Terra

Terra is a spherical world with natural geography and built features. This glossary defines the shared language used to describe that world.

## Language

**Landform**:
The broad shape of the ground, such as a valley, hills, mountains, or a plateau. A mountain landform can support a forest biome.
_Avoid_: Biome when referring to ground shape.

**Biome**:
An environment characterized by its climate and ecological cover, such as forest, desert, or tundra.
_Avoid_: Landform; biome as a catch-all for water and shore categories.

**Terrain type**:
The local surface category, including biomes, water, and shores, such as forest, river, or beach.
_Avoid_: Biome when referring to the full set of surface categories.

**Region**:
A named, contiguous geographic area, such as a particular forest or mountain range. Regions of different kinds may overlap, so a location can belong to a forest, a mountain range, and a road region at once; regions of the same kind remain separate, except that junctions belong to every road meeting there.
_Avoid_: Biome or terrain type when referring to a particular named area.

**Settlement**:
A place where people establish a community, including towns, villages, and outposts.
_Avoid_: Town as a general term for all settlements.

**Town**:
A larger settlement with mixed residential and shared uses.
_Avoid_: Town as a general term for all settlements.

**Village**:
A smaller residential settlement with an agricultural character.
_Avoid_: Outpost when referring to a small residential community.

**Outpost**:
A small settlement serving a defensive or watchkeeping purpose.
_Avoid_: Village when referring to a defensive site.

**Road network**:
The connected system of roads, bridges, and their endpoints through which settlements can be reached.
_Avoid_: Road when referring to the entire network.

**Road**:
A named travel connection between consecutive endpoints: junctions, settlement entrances, or bridge entrances. Each connection has its own identity and name, including roads inside settlements.
_Avoid_: Route when implying one identity continues through several roads and bridges.

**Junction**:
A place where roads physically join, including a fork or crossroads, and where each meeting road ends. Roads crossing at different heights without a travel connection do not form a junction.
_Avoid_: Crossing when physical connectivity is unclear.

**Settlement entrance**:
A distinct access point connecting roads outside a settlement to roads within it.
_Avoid_: Settlement center when referring to an access point.

**Bridge entrance**:
Either endpoint of a bridge, where it connects to the adjoining road network.
_Avoid_: Bridge when referring to only one endpoint.

**Bridge**:
A named structure providing a travel connection across an obstacle between its two entrances. It has its own identity, separate from the roads meeting those entrances.
_Avoid_: Road when referring specifically to the crossing structure.

**Water body**:
A distinct body of water, such as a lake, river, or ocean, that retains its identity when partly or wholly frozen.
_Avoid_: Water phase when referring to the body itself.

**Water phase**:
The local state of water as liquid or frozen. Different parts of one water body may have different phases.
_Avoid_: Water body or terrain type when referring to whether water is frozen.

**Surface condition**:
The local condition of a surface, such as normal or frozen, independent of its terrain type. Frozen ground has a frozen surface condition without being a water body.
_Avoid_: Water phase when referring to frozen ground.

**Scenery**:
Environmental objects distinct from structures, such as trees, rocks, mushrooms, shells, and snowmen. Flora is the plant-life subset of scenery.
_Avoid_: Flora for the entire category; props.

**Flora**:
The plant life of the world, such as trees, grasses, and flowers.
_Avoid_: Scenery when specifically referring to plant life.

**Structure**:
A built construction serving a practical purpose, such as a house, bridge, well, or fence. Human-made decorative objects such as snowmen are scenery rather than structures.
_Avoid_: Scenery; props.
